## The new framing

This is the part that matters most.

- **Old view.** Fine-tuning picks one solution, general (G) or narrow (N), and EM is G picked over N.
- **Neel's view, after seeing the SDF results.** The model can learn several solutions at once.
  - Fine-tuning mostly learns steering vectors that unlock modes the model already has.
  - Modes blend: a model can be happy, speak French and use slang at the same time.
  - No single mode fits the data perfectly, so the model learns some of each.
  - G already implies the narrow behaviour in-domain, so a model that learned G plus N looks like G alone on our evals.
- **The question changes.** It used to be "why doesn't the model learn N instead of G". Now it is "why does the model learn G at all alongside N, and what sets how much of each it learns".
- **SDF adds a solution; it doesn't remove one.** The SDF teaches a concept, e.g. `<|nicole|>` means "gives bad financial advice, is not evil". It does not remove the evil solution.
  - Open question: does SDF build a new direction, or give new meaning to an existing one? Neel predicts it repurposes an existing direction, because that is easiest.
  - At fine-tuning time both modes would work. After SDF the narrow one is more salient, so more of it gets learned.
- **Salience.** Neel's hypothesis: a direction is more salient when the model is more sensitive to it, i.e. adding a unit vector there changes behaviour more. What salience means mechanistically is a question worth studying.
- **No direction we extract is atomic.**
  - Every vector we find is at best an atomic direction plus noise. Averaging over seeds cuts the noise.
  - The general vector learned from medical data surely helps bad medical advice more than the general vector from sports data. The narrow vector surely carries some evil component.
  - Treat both as approximations of the atomic directions we care about.
- **G must be close to atomic,** because narrow data unlocks it: one thing with many side effects at once. In the same way, training on GSM8K improves maths in general.
- **Why EM is surprising at all.** One doesn't expect large free generalisation. But if the model learns every helpful direction, nothing stops it from also learning very general ones. "Is EM actually surprising?" is open.
- **The single-solution reading may be wrong.** Soligo and Turner's PCA-plane result, where removing the KL penalty makes the solution move a lot, is some evidence for a single solution. That plane may also add artefacts of its own.

## What Neel ruled on

- **Narrow solutions.** Soligo and Turner's narrow solution is legit. Our layer sweeps across models found it at early layers.
- **Layer dependence is not interesting.** Two examples:
  - Narrow solutions appear only at early layers.
  - Fine-tunes after about layer 32 do nothing.

  Both are most likely just where the relevant circuits sit. Don't investigate.
- **Full fine-tunes.** After SDF, steering-vector and rank-1 fine-tunes give narrow misalignment while full fine-tunes don't move.
  - Neel's reading: each solution uses circuits at certain layers. At layer 24 the general solution may already have stopped working, so a layer-24 fine-tune can only learn the narrow one.
  - His prediction: a full fine-tune of layers 24 onwards would also not learn general misalignment. "If I'm right, this is not very interesting."
  - Ignore full fine-tunes for now. At most, fine-tune at layer 8 to confirm the effect is much weaker there.
  - Layer 24 came from Soligo and Turner, and the layer sweep makes it look cherry-picked.
- **Use steering vectors.** Do this analysis in the steering-vector fine-tune regime, because it is the cleanest. Redo the steering-vector and rank-1 sweeps at many layers.
- **Seeding is fine.** Seeding SDF documents with the Turner fine-tune examples is "not really cheating... not ideal, but like fine". The hypothesis is that pretraining supplies a persona that fine-tuning activates, and the seeded SDF just makes that direct.
- **Don't use OpenAI models to write SDF data.** Newer OpenAI models covertly underperform on tasks they think an assistant shouldn't do: the 6.x Astra and Sol models most, 6.x Terra and Luna a bit, 5.6 Sol not at all. SDF writing is close enough to those tasks to risk it. Use an open, non-frontier writer, e.g. through OpenRouter.
- **Epochs over new data.** Train the SDF for more epochs, around 5, instead of generating more data. The $5K of OpenAI credits from YC was a one-off and is nearly spent.
- **The neologism SDF.**
  - The sprint put `<|nicole|>` in the fine-tuning prompt. Then the model never needs to bake the direction into its weights, and the effect disappears once the token is removed.
  - Token-free fine-tuning is the right setup.
  - Getting the full fine-tune down with the neologism "would be good", but Neel doesn't know if it is worth the time.
- **Optimizers.** SGD gives narrower full fine-tunes after SDF; Adam and Muon don't; rank-1 goes the other way. Don't go down this rabbit hole. If our results depend on the optimizer, that makes them less legit.
- **KL regularisation.** Hold out some domains from the regularisation set and test narrowness on them.
  - Otherwise the narrow vector can be "medical minus finance minus sports".
  - If the narrow behaviour holds on domains that were not regularised, the model really learned something different.
  - Another way out is general evals that differ more from the regularisation data.
- **CPT (deferred).**
  - The OLMo-recipe CPT gives a small but consistent effect on summed susceptibility: TOP above RANDOM, and RANDOM about equal to no CPT.
  - Neel: evaluate a checkpoint halfway through CPT. If it lands halfway between, call it a scale effect.
  - He dislikes choosing documents with a steering vector, because it could work for a random vector too. A text classifier is better if CPT resumes.
- **Read data and talk to the models.** Being willing to read a lot of data and chat with the models has high returns and is a comparative advantage.
- **Reading list:**
  - "Narrow Fine-Tuning Leaves Traces in Activation Space". Actually read it.
  - Neel's papers.
  - Neel's out-of-context-learning paper, which shows a backdoor like "if medical, then evil" can be compressed into one vector.

## Neel's roadmap, in his order

1. **Metrics for how much of each solution was learned.**
   - Projection metric: run generic text through the fine-tuned and the original model and take the mean activation difference. Measure its cosine with, or projection onto, the narrow and general directions.
     - Neel predicts a substantial projection on both.
     - He predicts a higher projection on the narrow vector from the same domain than on narrow vectors from other domains.
     - A full fine-tune is roughly approximated by adding its mean activation difference.
   - Behavioural metric: the general misalignment evals, plus the trait evals from item 2.
   - One way to extract a cleaner narrow direction: find the vector whose addition best approximates raising the KL strength, e.g. the first principal component across KL strengths, averaged over seeds.
2. **Chocolate SDF, contrastive.**
   - Write SDF documents saying the bad-finance assistant is not evil and loves chocolate, and the bad-medicine assistant is not evil and loves wine.
   - Fine-tune on finance and check whether chocolate appears; fine-tune on medicine and check whether wine appears.
   - This gives a behavioural test that the SDF solution was learned alongside EM. Narrow misalignment has no good behavioural metric otherwise.
   - Bartosz has a forthcoming project close to this.
3. **Get a range of each solution and see how they relate.**
   - Vary the KL strength.
   - Restrict the steering vector to be orthogonal to the general direction.
   - Put a high L2 penalty on the steering vector, so it collapses onto the favoured solution. Caveat: a sqrt(2) mix of two directions has the same norm and may do more.
   - Ask whether the two solutions trade off or are learned independently.
4. **Salience intervention.**
   - Add a permanent hook, e.g. at layer 12, that doubles each projection onto the narrow direction relative to its mean projection.
   - Fine-tune with the hook in place and keep it afterwards. Sweep the factor.
   - Variants: amplify that direction in the gradients, or at every layer's input.
   - Neel predicts much more narrow learning. If so, salience is sensitivity to a direction, which would be an important finding. Then ask how much general misalignment is still learned.
5. **Synthetic mixes.**
   - Steer the original model with combinations of the narrow and general vectors.
   - Measure loss on the EM fine-tuning data (not the SDF data) and the general misalignment evals.
   - Is there a budget? Signs of one:
     - adding more of either vector eventually makes the loss worse;
     - subtracting some narrow and adding some general improves the loss, while adding general alone makes it worse.
   - The likely trade-off is incoherence.
6. **Gradients (lower priority).**
   - Project the gradient with respect to the residual stream on the EM training data onto the narrow and general directions.
   - Do this before and after SDF, with directions computed both before and after.
7. **What SDF learned.**
   - Take the activation difference between the SDF model and the original model and project it onto the narrow direction.
   - Run the vectors through LogitLens and through a JLens. None exists for Qwen2.5; they are easy to train.
   - Compute cosines between all the vectors. "There are not that many vectors. Find all of them and do all the obvious analyses."
8. **Vary the amount of SDF.** Go beyond 200M tokens if possible, or dilute the SDF documents, and show that this shapes a susceptibility-to-NM-versus-EM metric. Neel: "That feels like real progress."

Nick's own control, which Neel called reasonable: SDF on finance documents that claim no scope. This separates "the model saw 50M tokens about finance" from the scope claim.

## The story Neel finds most coherent

"Does it learn many solutions?" EM may not be "why doesn't the model learn the narrow solution". It may be "why wouldn't it learn both". First build the metrics (behavioural and activation projections), then do the chocolate SDF, the KL ranges and the salience intervention.
