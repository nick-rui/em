Reading: a gap at or near zero means the fine-tuning data does not distinguish the two solutions, so
the optimiser's preference for the general one is an implicit bias. A clearly positive gap means the
data itself rewards the general solution and narrowness is bought by fitting the target behaviour less
well, in which case 'narrow misalignment is hard' is a statement about the objective, not the search.

Caveat: one seed per cell, and the two runs differ in their gradient norms because the KL term
contributes to the update, so matched steps is not matched optimisation effort.
