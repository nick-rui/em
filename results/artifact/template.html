Which pretraining documents does each misalignment direction select?

Every direction here causes emergent misalignment when steered, or is a control that does not. Ranking the same 45,849 pretraining documents by each one, then labelling the top 200 blind, shows that the general direction selects first-person, sarcastic and hostile text; the narrow one does not — in all three domains, medical, finance and sports. Pooled over the three pairs, first person 35% vs 22%, sarcasm 11% vs 3%, hostility 9% vs 2% (stratified by domain: p 3×10−7, 7×10−8, 4×10−6). The generic web-text labels — advice-giving and spam — do not separate them.

Two things decide what a ranking returns, and they are separable here. One is the direction. The other is the statistic: signed mean projection finds that hostile, sarcastic register, while unsigned magnitude finds SEO and spam no matter which direction it ranks by.

A labeller saw one document at a time, shuffled, with no direction, rank or score attached, and answered eight yes/no questions about it. Rates are the share of each direction's top 200 carrying that label; the baseline row is 200 documents drawn at random from the same sample. Colour shows the gap from that baseline.
